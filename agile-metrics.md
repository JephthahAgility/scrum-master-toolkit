Agile Metrics & Jira


Purpose


Agile metrics help Scrum teams understand their delivery patterns, identify areas for improvement, and make work more visible and predictable.

Metrics should be used as tools for learning and improvement rather than as a way to measure individual performance.

1. Velocity
What is Velocity?
Velocity measures the amount of estimated work a Scrum team completes during a Sprint.

It is commonly expressed in Story Points.

Example
If a team completes:

Sprint 1: 70 Story Points
Sprint 2: 75 Story Points
Sprint 3: 80 Story Points
Sprint 4: 72 Story Points
Sprint 5: 81 Story Points
The average velocity is:

(70 + 75 + 80 + 72 + 81) ÷ 5 = 75.6 Story Points

How a Scrum Master can use it
Velocity can help with:

Sprint planning
Capacity discussions
Release planning
Identifying changes in team delivery patterns
Velocity should not be used as a target or as a measure of individual productivity.

2. Cycle Time
What is Cycle Time?
Cycle Time measures how long it takes a work item to move through the team's active development process until it reaches the team's defined completion point.

Example
If a story enters In Progress on Monday and reaches the team's agreed completion status on Friday:

Cycle Time = 4 working days

How a Scrum Master can use it
Cycle Time can help identify:

Bottlenecks
Work that is taking longer than expected
Process improvements
Changes in delivery flow
3. Lead Time
What is Lead Time?
Lead Time measures the time from when work is requested or enters the team's workflow until it is completed.

Lead Time therefore generally includes time before active development begins.

Example
A request enters the backlog on June 1 and is completed on June 15.

Lead Time = 14 calendar days

Lead Time vs Cycle Time
Metric	Measures
Lead Time	Request/start of workflow → Completion
Cycle Time	Active work begins → Completion
The exact start and end points should be clearly defined by the team.

4. Throughput
What is Throughput?
Throughput measures the number of work items completed during a specific period.

Example
A team completes:

Week 1: 8 items
Week 2: 10 items
Week 3: 7 items
Week 4: 11 items
The team's average weekly throughput is:

36 ÷ 4 = 9 items per week

How a Scrum Master can use it
Throughput can help teams understand:

Delivery capacity
Workflow trends
Changes in demand
The effect of process improvements
5. Work in Progress (WIP)
What is WIP?
Work in Progress represents items that have started but are not yet completed.

Example
If a team currently has:

4 items in Development
3 items in Testing
2 items awaiting review
Then:

WIP = 9 items

Why WIP matters
Too much WIP can contribute to:

Context switching
Longer cycle times
Bottlenecks
Work becoming stale
A Scrum Master can help the team focus on finishing existing work before starting too much new work.

6. Aging Work Items
What is an Aging Work Item?
An aging work item is an item that has remained in progress for a certain amount of time without being completed.

Example
A story has been in progress for 8 days while the team's typical cycle time is approximately 5 days.

The Scrum Master may investigate:

Is there a blocker?
Is the story too large?
Is another team needed?
Is testing delayed?
Are requirements unclear?
Important Principle
Aging should be used to encourage conversations about flow and impediments, not to blame individuals.

7. Jira Metrics
Jira can provide visibility into Agile delivery through boards, reports, dashboards, and filters.

Examples include:

Velocity Chart
Control Chart
Cumulative Flow Diagram
Sprint Report
Burndown Chart
Burnup Chart
Average Age / Aging Work Items
Cycle Time
Lead Time
Scrum Master Responsibilities
A Scrum Master can use Jira to:

Monitor Sprint progress
Identify blocked or aging work
Review delivery trends
Facilitate data-informed conversations
Improve transparency
Support stakeholder communication
Identify opportunities for continuous improvement
8. Using Metrics Responsibly
Metrics should provide context rather than create pressure.

A Scrum Master should consider:

Trends over time rather than isolated numbers
Team context
Changes in scope
Changes in team composition
Dependencies and external blockers
Quality and customer outcomes
No single metric provides a complete picture of team performance.

9. Example Scrum Master Metrics Review
During a Sprint review or team discussion, a Scrum Master might examine:

Metric	Example	Possible Discussion
Velocity	75.6 SP	What delivery pattern are we seeing?
Cycle Time	6 days	Where can flow improve?
Throughput	9 items/week	Is throughput changing?
WIP	12 items	Are we starting too much work?
Aging Items	2 items > 7 days	Are there blockers or dependencies?
Lead Time	12 days	Where is work waiting before completion?
The goal is to use the information to support transparency, inspection, adaptation, and continuous improvement.

Key Takeaway
Agile metrics are most useful when they help a team understand its system of work and identify opportunities for improvement.

A Scrum Master's role is to facilitate conversations around the data and help the team turn observations into meaningful improvements.
